The legacy

A story that started in Tokyo

Brizon Gym is not just a name on a T-shirt. It is a line of masters that runs from the karate dojos of 1950s Japan to the kick-boxing rings of Amsterdam, and from there to France.

01

Kenji Kurosaki & the birth of kick-boxing

Sensei Kenji Kurosaki was born into a traditional samurai family. In 1953 he trained in the Goju-ryu karate of the Yamaguchi school alongside Mas Oyama. Together they created a new style, Kyokushin kaikan: Oyama handled public relations, while Kurosaki, who stayed in the background, was the instructor and the real fighter.

Kick-boxing itself was born when Nihon Kenpo karate practitioner Tatsuo Yamada became interested in Muay Thai. In 1962 he held his first sport karate event, and boxing promoter Osamu Noguchi, impressed by a Muay Thai show, wanted to organise karate against Muay Thai.

In 1963 the Oyama dojo sent three fighters to Bangkok: Kenji Kurosaki, Tadashi Nakamura and Akio Fujihira. Kurosaki was the only one to lose, but he went on to become one of the greatest coaches in kick-boxing history. In 1966 the first kick-boxing organisation, the Japan Kickboxing Federation, was born, and the same year Kurosaki travelled to Holland at the invitation of his student Jon Bluming. He is considered the father of Dutch kick-boxing.

Kurosaki left Kyokushin and founded the Mejiro Gym in Tokyo's Mejiro district (today the Kurosaki Dojo). It produced some of the most important talent in kick-boxing history.

02

Jan Plas & the Mejiro style

In 1975 the Dutchman Jan Plas visited Japan and trained with Sensei Kurosaki in Tokyo. So impressed by the school, he stayed to train, then opened his own Mejiro Gym in Amsterdam, a branch of the Japanese gym.

It was there that the famous Mejiro style was developed and perfected. It was tested and refined in many Muay Thai fights in Thailand, where Mejiro students spent years training and competing. Its best-known champion is Rob Kaman.

Because the Mejiro name already existed in Europe with Jan Plas, the French branch of this family would grow under another name: Brizon.

03

Patrick Brizon, the pioneer

Born in 1951, Patrick Brizon started judo at 12, then Viet Vo Dao, and at 19 karate, which he went on to teach in Clermont-Ferrand.

In 1975 he travelled to Japan to train with Master Yamaguchi Gogen. One evening, in his hotel room, he discovered kick-boxing on television by chance. Full-contact fighting won him over instantly. The next day he walked into Sensei Kurosaki's dojo, where Toshio Fujiwara, Tabata, Shima and other champions of the land of the rising sun trained. The kick-boxing practised there was Muay Thai under another name.

The training was so hard that every other European quit before the end of the course, every one except Patrick. He had his first fight in Tokyo and won it by knockout in the first round against a Japanese fighter.

Back in France he opened the first kick-boxing (Muay Thai) school in the country, the Kurosaki Dojo in Clermont-Ferrand, and brought his best karate students into this new discipline that allowed full contact and the KO. The victories followed one another and he became the first French European champion (1978/79). He founded three Brizon Gyms in Auvergne: Vichy, Riom and Clermont-Ferrand.

04

Surviving: the Brizon Gym lives on

Patrick took a three-year break from competition for health reasons and devoted himself to teaching Muay Thai, then fiercely returned to the ring. In November 1982 he died of a heart attack while jogging. He was 31. Thai boxing in France was seven years old, and it had to go on.

The day after his death a new era began. His students gave lessons in Riom, Saint-Mandé, Cannes and Vichy, and the gyms took the name of their master: Brizon Gym. In Riom, Gérard Dimier passed on the Brizon knowledge and spirit by creating the Brizon Gym Riomois, a club where some 250 members practise Muay Thai, for leisure or competition, across 8 classes a week.

The keeper of the legacy

Didier Le Borgne

“Il Maestro dei Campioni”: born in France in 1959, one of the European pioneers of kick-boxing and Muay Thai.

At 13, in the wave of Bruce Lee's films, Didier began karate under Master Patrick Brizon. When Patrick came back from Tokyo and led his best karate students into kick-boxing, Didier was completely fascinated by this more realistic form of fighting, at a time when full-contact competition was still forbidden in France.

He decided to combine a fighting career with teaching, with constant trips to Thailand and above all to Holland, where Jan Plas became his master. Didier learned a great deal from Jan Plas, training with his best students (Kaman, Brilleman, Royers, El Geubli, Yamali and many others), and Jan Plas taught him the foundations of coaching.

In those days there was only one international organisation, so only one European champion: the strongest. Didier fought for the European title in a rematch against the Dutchman Lucien Carbin, whom he had beaten hard months earlier. Carbin won by KO in the first round, and he remains the only Dutchman ever to beat Didier.

With the Mejiro Gym of Amsterdam, Jan Plas took Didier to Hong Kong, Japan, America, Canada and England to fight successfully for Team Mejiro, the strongest and most famous team of the 1980s.

In 1989 he fought his last Muay Thai bout, against Soudareth, now a friend, whom Didier calls one of the smartest fighters he ever faced. He also boxed professionally; one fight, filmed by Canal+, he won by KO in the third round. In 1995 he fought for the WAKO-PRO world full-contact title in Rio de Janeiro against Francisco Veras. Driven by curiosity and personal challenge, in 1998 and 1999 he fought two Vale Tudo bouts at Sergio Batarelli's prestigious IVC in São Paulo, winning the first on his feet and losing the second by submission to a student of Carlson Gracie.

Throughout, he trained countless fighters wearing the colours of the Brizon Gym. Today he trains professional K-1, Muay Thai and MMA (stand-up) fighters and helps organisers build fight cards.

Fighters he has coached include Cyril Abidi, Irina Mazepa, Jérôme Turcan, Stéphane Nikiéma, Djamel Rahal and many more. He is invited to give seminars all over Italy and France, and to international championships.
